What is MIC + B12?

MIC is an injectable supplement blend combining vitamin B12 with three lipotropic (fat-metabolizing) compounds: methionine, inositol, and choline. It helps boost energy levels, support metabolism, and assist with weight loss.

How does MIC + B12 work?

Methionine (M): An essential amino acid that helps the liver break down fats and prevents extra fat from building up in the body.

Inositol (I): A compound related to B-vitamins that helps control insulin and assists in moving fats through the bloodstream.

Choline (C): A nutrient that helps transport fat out of the liver so your body can use it as fuel.

Vitamin B12: A vitamin that helps form red blood cells, reduces fatigue, and boosts overall energy levels.

Questions we hear every day

What is the difference between Methylcobalamin and cyanocobalamin?

Methylcobalamin and cyanocobalamin are two forms of vitamin B12 that support healthy red blood cell production, nerve function, and energy metabolism. Methylcobalamin is a naturally occurring, biologically active form that can be used readily by the body, while cyanocobalamin is a synthetic, highly stable form that the body converts into active B12. Both can effectively restore B12 levels, although methylcobalamin is often preferred in wellness treatments because it is already in an active form.

Does MIC+B12 support detoxification?

MIC-B12 supports detoxification primarily through its lipotropic compounds—Methionine, Inositol, and Choline—which help the liver process and eliminate fats and toxins more efficiently. These nutrients work together to break down fat deposits in the liver, prevent fat buildup, and assist in transporting waste products out of the body.

What are the benefits of MIC+B12?

Many people report improvements in mental clarity, focus, and mood after consistent use—especially if they were previously deficient in B12. Inositol, another component of MIC-B12, may also support brain function and emotional balance, particularly in individuals with insulin sensitivity or hormonal imbalances.

Can MIC+B12 interact with other medications?

MIC+B12 injections generally have few significant medication interactions, but patients should still disclose all prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, and supplements before treatment because formulations may vary. Chloramphenicol may reduce the blood-cell response to vitamin B12, while metformin and long-term acid-reducing medications can lower B12 levels—although this is primarily an absorption issue and is less relevant to injectable B12. Patients with a cobalt or B12 allergy, kidney or liver disease, or Leber’s hereditary optic neuropathy should be evaluated carefully before treatment.
