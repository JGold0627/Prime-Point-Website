Your consultation is focused on building a personalized treatment plan

This is a clinical strategy session. You will meet with a Prime Point provider whose role is to evaluate your goals, health history, lifestyle, available labs, and physiology, then use clinical judgment to discuss what may be appropriate for you. Recommendations are based on safety, alignment, and medical appropriateness, not a one-size-fits-all menu of therapies. Not every option is right for every person. Thoughtful selection matters, and your plan can focus on practical next steps that fit where you are now. Your provider may also outline education, follow-up priorities, monitoring considerations, or prescription options only when clinically appropriate.

Tap into the essentials that make your consult more useful: what gets reviewed, what questions matter, and what can happen next.

Prescriptions Are peptides prescribed automatically?

No. Your provider reviews your health history, medications, goals, risks, and eligibility before any prescription is considered.

Appropriateness How does a provider decide?

The decision is based on your goals, medical history, medications, risks, contraindications, available labs, and clinical judgment.

Your Info What may be reviewed?

Goals, health history, medications, allergies, lifestyle, prior labs, contraindications, and your questions may all be part of the review.

Labs Will labs be required?

It depends. Existing labs may be reviewed, and additional labs may be recommended when they would support a safer care decision.

Beyond Rx What can a consult include?

A consult may include education, lifestyle guidance, monitoring priorities, follow-up needs, or prescription options only when clinically appropriate.

Risks What limitations should be discussed?

The conversation should cover fit, possible side effects, medication conflicts, follow-up needs, and realistic expectations.

Eligibility Who may not be a candidate?

Some people may not be a fit based on medical history, current medications, contraindications, risks, or provider judgment.

Next Steps What happens after review?

Your provider can discuss reasonable next steps, which may include education, labs, follow-up, monitoring, or a different care path.

Information on this site is educational and is not a diagnosis, treatment claim, or guarantee of results. Peptide therapies require clinical review, may not be appropriate for everyone, and are not guaranteed after a consult. Prescriptions are considered only when clinically appropriate.
