Health information and HIPAA privacy practices

Effective date of these health-information provisions: October 11, 2026.

These provisions describe how Protected Health Information (“PHI”) may be used and disclosed, the privacy rights that apply to that information, and the responsibilities applicable to our handling of PHI. PHI is individually identifiable health information protected by the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (“HIPAA”). Information collected through a general website is not automatically PHI; its legal protection depends on the information, who holds it, and the circumstances.

Prime Point Health provides a technology and administrative platform. Clinical care is provided by Beluga Health, P.A. or other affiliated professional entities, as described in our Terms of Service. The treating provider, pharmacy, or other covered healthcare entity is responsible for its own Notice of Privacy Practices. That notice identifies the organization responsible for your clinical records and explains its uses, disclosures, duties, and patient rights. You may request that notice from the organization providing your care or contact our Privacy Team for assistance identifying the appropriate privacy contact.

When we create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI on behalf of a covered healthcare entity, our handling of that information is subject to the HIPAA requirements applicable to our role, our business associate obligations, and that entity’s privacy practices. We are required to safeguard PHI, limit its use and disclosure to permitted purposes, and meet applicable breach-reporting obligations. A covered entity must maintain the privacy of PHI, provide notice of its legal duties and practices, and follow its effective Notice of Privacy Practices. These website provisions supplement, and do not replace, the treating entity’s notice.

For PHI, these health-information provisions and applicable law control over any broader website, advertising, analytics, sharing, or business-transfer language elsewhere in this policy. Acceptance of this Privacy Policy does not by itself authorize a use or disclosure of PHI that requires a separate authorization.

Uses and disclosures of PHI

The following categories describe uses and disclosures that may be permitted or required under HIPAA. A category listed here is not unrestricted permission to disclose information. Each disclosure must satisfy applicable legal conditions, any more protective state law, and the responsible covered entity’s notice. When Prime Point Health acts on behalf of a covered entity, we may handle PHI only as allowed by our role and applicable agreements or as required by law.

  • Treatment. PHI may be used or shared to provide, coordinate, or manage healthcare and related services, including communications among treating providers, referrals, and pharmacy coordination.
  • Payment. PHI may be used or shared to obtain payment for healthcare services, including billing, collection, and, when relevant to the service, coverage verification and communications with health plans or other providers. This provision does not mean that a particular Prime Point Health service accepts insurance.
  • Healthcare operations. PHI may be used or shared for quality assessment and improvement, case management, accreditation, licensing, credentialing, medical review, audits, and legal or administrative services.
  • As required by law. PHI may be disclosed when required by applicable federal, state, or local law, within the limits of that requirement.
  • Public health and safety. Permitted disclosures may include preventing or controlling disease, injury, or disability; reporting abuse or neglect; reporting medication reactions or product problems; and notifying people of a communicable-disease exposure or risk, when legally authorized.
  • Health oversight activities. PHI may be disclosed to authorized oversight agencies for audits, investigations, inspections, licensure, and other legally authorized oversight.
  • Judicial and administrative proceedings. PHI may be disclosed in response to a court or administrative order or other lawful process. A subpoena or discovery request is subject to applicable notice, protective-order, authorization, or other legal requirements; it does not automatically permit release of all records.
  • Law enforcement. PHI may be disclosed for legally permitted law-enforcement purposes, including required reporting of certain wounds or injuries and compliance with qualifying legal process.
  • Research. PHI may be used or disclosed for research with your authorization or another legally permitted basis, such as an approved institutional review board or privacy board waiver, with required privacy protections.
  • Organ and tissue donation. PHI may be disclosed to authorized organizations involved in organ procurement, transplantation, or donation as permitted by law.
  • Workers’ compensation. PHI may be disclosed as authorized for workers’ compensation or similar programs addressing work-related injuries or illnesses.
  • Military and veterans. PHI of members of the armed forces may be disclosed to appropriate military authorities for purposes permitted by law.
  • Inmates and persons in custody. PHI may be disclosed to a correctional institution or official with lawful custody when permitted for healthcare, health and safety, or other authorized correctional purposes.

Where the minimum-necessary standard applies, the information used, requested, or disclosed must be limited to what is reasonably necessary for the permitted purpose. Additional protections for sensitive records are described below.

Authorization and sensitive records

Uses or disclosures of PHI that are not otherwise permitted or required by law require your written authorization. This includes uses or disclosures for marketing, sales of PHI, and most disclosures of psychotherapy notes when HIPAA requires authorization. General website consent, a cookie preference, or a marketing opt-out is not a substitute for a required HIPAA authorization.

You may revoke an authorization in writing by contacting the organization that obtained it or our Privacy Team for help routing the request. Revocation applies prospectively, subject to actions already taken in reliance on the authorization and other applicable legal exceptions.

HIV-related information, mental health records, genetic information, reproductive-health information, and substance use disorder records may have additional federal or state protections. When applicable law requires a separate or more specific consent, that consent must be obtained before disclosure unless a legal exception applies. Substance use disorder records covered by 42 CFR Part 2 are subject to that law’s additional restrictions, including restrictions on use in proceedings against the patient.

Your rights regarding PHI

The following rights apply to PHI under HIPAA, subject to the law’s exceptions and any additional protections that apply. Send a written request to the provider or other covered entity maintaining the records, or contact our Privacy Team for assistance. We may verify your identity and authority and will handle requests within our responsibility or help route them to the appropriate privacy officer or records custodian.

Inspect and obtain a copy

You may request access to, or an electronic or paper copy of, PHI in the designated record set maintained about you, with certain exceptions. An electronic copy may be requested in a readily producible format or another agreed format. Any copying or delivery fee must be reasonable, cost-based, and permitted by applicable law. The responsible entity must explain a denial and any available review rights as required by law.

Request an amendment

You may request correction or amendment of PHI you believe is inaccurate or incomplete. Identify the information and explain why it should be changed. Requests may be denied on grounds allowed by law, such as when the information is accurate and complete or was created by another entity that remains available to address the request. A denial must include the required explanation and information about your right to submit a statement of disagreement.

Obtain an accounting of disclosures

You may request an accounting of certain disclosures of your PHI during the preceding six years, or a shorter period you specify. Disclosures for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations and certain other disclosures are excluded as permitted by HIPAA. The responsible entity must apply the applicable rules governing the first free accounting and any permitted fees for additional requests.

Request restrictions

You may request limits on the use or disclosure of PHI for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations, specifying the information, restriction, and recipient involved. A covered entity generally is not required to agree, except where the law requires it. If you pay for a healthcare item or service out of pocket in full, you may require the provider to restrict disclosure of that information to your health plan for payment or healthcare operations, unless disclosure is required by law. This HIPAA protection applies nationwide, not only in California.

Request confidential communications

You may request communications about your PHI by a particular method or at a particular location, such as a different telephone number or mailing address. Tell the responsible provider or privacy contact how or where you wish to be contacted. Covered healthcare providers must accommodate reasonable requests.

Receive a paper copy of the notice

You may request a paper copy of this policy and the applicable healthcare entity’s Notice of Privacy Practices even if you previously agreed to receive information electronically. Contact our Privacy Team for this policy or help obtaining the treating entity’s notice.

Receive breach notification

You have the right to receive notice of a breach of your unsecured PHI when notification is required by HIPAA or applicable state law. We will meet the reporting and notification duties applicable to our role, including notifying the responsible covered entity when we handle PHI on its behalf. The responsible entity must provide required individual notices within the time limits imposed by law.

Exercise rights through an authorized representative

A legally authorized personal representative may exercise rights on your behalf, subject to verification of authority and applicable exceptions. You may also use the complaint process described below without retaliation.

Transmission and protection of PHI

Our handling of electronic PHI is subject to the HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rules, including applicable provisions of 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164, and other applicable privacy and security laws. PHI must be protected with the administrative, physical, and technical safeguards required for our role.

Our policy is to use protected transmission methods for PHI, including encryption through current Transport Layer Security (TLS), the successor to SSL, or equivalent safeguards appropriate to the transmission and applicable security requirements. Access controls, recipient verification, and other reasonable safeguards are also important to protect confidentiality and integrity. No technology can guarantee that every transmission or storage system will be free from security risks.

Ordinary email and text messaging may not be encrypted. You may request a confidential or more secure communication method. If you specifically request a copy of your PHI by unencrypted email, the responsible entity must first explain the risk that another person could read or access the information and confirm that you still want that delivery method. Merely emailing a records request does not automatically authorize unencrypted delivery. See HHS guidance on requested delivery methods.

Please use the clinical provider’s secure patient communication channel for sensitive health details where available. You can contact our Privacy Team to request assistance arranging a suitable delivery method.

State-specific medical-privacy protections

HIPAA protections apply to covered information throughout the United States. Applicable state laws may impose additional limits on uses and disclosures, additional consent requirements, faster access rights, or stronger security and breach-notification protections. Where a more protective law applies, we follow that requirement for information within our responsibility. Applicability depends on the information, the entity holding it, the service, and the relevant jurisdiction, rather than residence alone.

The following examples supplement the PHI provisions above. They are distinct from the consumer-privacy rights described in the California and Nevada sections below. Written consent will be obtained when the applicable law requires it, including for treatment, payment, or operations disclosures when those purposes do not qualify for a legal exception.

California

The Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA) and other applicable California laws may protect medical information in addition to HIPAA. These protections include limits on disclosure and on certain marketing uses or sales of medical information. Required authorization must be obtained before those uses or disclosures unless an applicable legal exception permits them. California patients may also have additional rights to inspect records and obtain copies, including an available electronic format, under applicable law.

The right to restrict disclosure to a health plan when an item or service has been paid for out of pocket in full is described in Your rights regarding PHI and is not limited to California. Where California law allows a minor to consent to sensitive services, including certain reproductive-health, mental-health, or substance use disorder services, parental access to the related records may be restricted. Required consent and legally permitted exceptions govern those disclosures.

New York

New York provides additional confidentiality protections for HIV-related information, certain mental health records, and genetic test records, including under Public Health Law Article 27-F, Mental Hygiene Law Section 33.13, and Civil Rights Law Section 79-l, when applicable. Disclosures must meet the relevant consent, confidentiality, and redisclosure rules. Written consent will be obtained where required, including when an otherwise permitted treatment, payment, or operations disclosure does not satisfy the applicable state-law exception.

Texas

The Texas Medical Records Privacy Act, including Health and Safety Code Chapter 181, may impose additional obligations concerning PHI, including notice and authorization requirements for electronic disclosure, with statutory exceptions. PHI may be disclosed electronically for the permitted purposes described in this policy; a separate authorization will be obtained when Texas law requires one. Applicable Texas protections for mental health records and applicable substance use disorder confidentiality rules also apply. Required safeguards extend to handling, storage, and disposal of protected information.

Florida

Applicable Florida laws provide confidentiality protections for medical records, mental health records, HIV/AIDS-related information, and substance use disorder treatment records. We follow the authorization requirements and disclosure exceptions applicable to the information we handle. Written consent will be obtained when required, including for treatment, payment, or operations disclosures that do not fall within a permitted exception. Electronic records are subject to applicable privacy and security safeguards.

Illinois

Applicable Illinois laws provide additional protection for mental health and developmental disability records, HIV/AIDS-related information, and genetic testing information. Required written consent and limits on use and redisclosure apply unless a statutory exception permits disclosure. We also comply with applicable Illinois breach-notification requirements when an incident involving information within our responsibility triggers those requirements.

Massachusetts

Applicable Massachusetts laws provide additional protections for mental health records, HIV test information, and genetic testing information. We obtain the specific written consent required for disclosure unless a legal exception applies. Applicable Massachusetts requirements for protecting personal information and electronic records also govern information within their scope.

Other states may provide additional protections. Contact our Privacy Team or the privacy officer of the organization maintaining your clinical records for assistance with rights that apply to your information. Further guidance on the relationship between HIPAA and more protective state laws is available from HHS.

Your California privacy rights

CCPA rights disclosure. If you are a California resident, the CCPA allows you to make certain requests about your personal information. Specifically, the CCPA allows you to request that we:

  • Inform you about the categories of personal information we collect or disclose about you; the categories of sources of such information; the business or commercial purpose for collecting your personal information; and the categories of third parties with whom we share or disclose personal information.
  • Provide access to and/or a copy of certain information we hold about you.
  • Delete certain information we have about you.

The CCPA further provides you with the right to not be discriminated against, as provided for in applicable law, for exercising your rights.

Certain information may be exempt from such requests under California law. For example, we need certain information in order to provide the Services to you. We also will take reasonable steps to verify your identity before responding to a request.

If you are a California resident and would like to exercise any of your data rights under California law, email us at Care@Joinprimepoint.com.

Please include your full name, email address, and residential address associated with your use of our Services, along with the rights you would like to exercise, so that we can process your request in an efficient manner. If you would like to receive a copy of your medical record in connection with your request, please indicate that in your email message. Sending a request by email does not automatically authorize delivery of your medical record by unencrypted email. If you request that delivery method, the responsible entity will explain the transmission risks and confirm your choice, as described in Transmission and protection of PHI.

Sale of personal information

Prime Point Health does not, and will not, sell information that identifies you, such as your name, email address, phone number, or postal address. Like many companies with an Internet presence, we do work with third parties that provide marketing and advertising services to us, including interest-based advertising services, and these third parties may place tags, pixels, cookies, beacons, and other similar tracking mechanisms on our Website and App and collect your online identifiers.

Because of the CCPA’s broad definitions of “sale” and “personal information,” making certain online identifiers available to these companies so that they can provide marketing and advertising services to Prime Point Health may be considered a “sale” under the CCPA.

To opt out of sharing your information with participating third parties for interest-based advertising, please visit the Network Advertising Initiative’s opt-out resources, the Digital Advertising Alliance’s Consumer Opt-Out tool, and Your Online Choices. These opt-outs are device and browser based, so you will need to opt out on each of your devices and browsers. For more information, see Online analytics and advertising above.
