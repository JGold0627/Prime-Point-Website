## Copy and source review

The copy describes endogenous glutathione's biochemical functions, rather than promising outcomes from injections. It does not add treatment timelines, disease prevention claims, or guaranteed results. This is an implementation source check, not medical or legal approval.

- Antioxidant defense: Glutathione is an antioxidant made naturally by your body. It helps neutralize reactive molecules and protects cells from oxidative stress.
  - Source: [National Cancer Institute, glutathione](https://www.cancer.gov/publications/dictionaries/cancer-drug/def/glutathione).
- Liver detoxification: In the liver, glutathione works with enzymes to bind certain unwanted compounds, helping the body process and remove them.
  - Sources: [NCBI, human GSTA1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene?LinkName=nuccore_gene&from_uid=183677), [Coles et al., human liver GSTA1 and busulfan conjugation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12087351/), [Czerwinski et al., busulfan conjugation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8886613/).
- Immune cell function: Immune cells use glutathione to manage oxidative stress. This balance helps support the normal activity of T cells, a key part of your immune system.
  - Source: [Mak et al., Glutathione Primes T Cell Metabolism for Inflammation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28423341/). This mechanistic study includes murine T cells; it is not evidence of improved clinical immunity from administered glutathione.
