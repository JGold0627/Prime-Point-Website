## Final card copy and sources

### Cellular energy

NAD+ helps cells turn nutrients into usable energy. By cycling between NAD+ and NADH, it carries electrons that help drive ATP production in mitochondria.

- [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: Niacin, Health Professional Fact Sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Niacin-HealthProfessional/): NAD in redox reactions and transfer of nutrient energy to ATP.
- [Fiedorczuk et al., Nature, 2016: Atomic structure of the entire mammalian mitochondrial complex I](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature19794): original structural research describing transfer of electrons from NADH coupled to proton translocation in mammalian mitochondrial complex I.
- [Zhu et al., Nature, 2016: Structure of mammalian respiratory complex I](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature19095): original research linking NADH-driven complex I activity to the gradient that powers ATP synthesis.

### DNA repair & maintenance

NAD+ is used by enzymes called PARPs and sirtuins. These enzymes help cells respond to DNA damage and coordinate repair and cellular stress responses.

- [Wilk et al., Scientific Reports, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31959836/): NAD+ as a substrate for PARPs and sirtuins; original cell-culture experiments on NAD+ availability and DNA repair.
- [Mao et al., Science, 2011: SIRT6 promotes DNA repair under stress by activating PARP1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21680843/): original mammalian-cell experiments on stress responses and repair.

### Daily rhythms

NAD+ levels and the cellular clock influence one another. Research links this daily cycle to SIRT1, an enzyme that helps coordinate the timing of cellular activity.

- [Nakahata et al., Science, 2009: Circadian control of the NAD+ salvage pathway by CLOCK-SIRT1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19286518/): original mouse-embryo-fibroblast research identifying a feedback loop between NAD+ metabolism, SIRT1, and circadian gene expression.

Sources accessed September 8, 2026. Copy is paraphrased. Daily-rhythm wording explicitly describes research on cellular activity rather than a clinical sleep outcome.
