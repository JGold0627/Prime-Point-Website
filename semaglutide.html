What is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist that may be discussed as part of a provider-guided weight-management consultation. Whether it is appropriate depends on an individual review of your health history, current medications, goals, and potential risks.

Prime Point begins with a medical intake and licensed-provider evaluation. If prescribed, the provider will explain the recommended plan, limitations, follow-up, and next steps. Semaglutide is not appropriate for everyone, prescriptions are not guaranteed, and results vary.

What is the difference between Semaglutide and Tirzepatide?

Semaglutide and Tirzepatide are different medications with different mechanisms, considerations, and potential risks. Semaglutide acts on GLP-1 receptors, while Tirzepatide acts on both GIP and GLP-1 receptors.

A licensed provider can review your medical history, current medications, goals, and questions before discussing whether either option may be clinically appropriate. The consultation does not guarantee eligibility or a prescription.

The science behind Semaglutide

GLP-1 is a naturally occurring hormone involved in blood-sugar regulation, digestion, and appetite signaling. After eating, it helps the body coordinate several processes related to glucose and fullness.

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. When clinically appropriate and prescribed, it acts at GLP-1 receptors and may influence appetite signaling and how quickly the stomach empties.

Individual response, tolerability, and potential side effects vary. A licensed provider should review your health history, medications, goals, and risks before discussing whether Semaglutide may be appropriate.

What might patients notice?

  • Changes in appetite or food-related thoughts
  • Smaller portions may feel more satisfying
  • Fullness may last longer after meals

Experiences differ, and these effects are not guaranteed.

Questions we hear every day

Do I need to make changes to my diet with Semaglutide?

Prime Point supports a sustainable, flexible approach to nutrition rather than an unnecessarily restrictive diet. Your provider may discuss nourishing food choices, adequate protein, hydration, meal size, and eating patterns based on your goals, health history, and how you tolerate treatment.

Individual needs differ. Avoid making major dietary changes or beginning a highly restrictive eating plan solely because of Semaglutide without first discussing it with your care team.

What side effects can occur with Semaglutide?

Possible side effects may include:

  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Diarrhea
  • Constipation
  • Abdominal pain or discomfort
  • Decreased appetite
  • Indigestion

Side effects and their severity vary, and other risks may be serious. Contact your healthcare professional if symptoms persist, worsen, or concern you, and seek urgent care for severe symptoms. Review the prescribing information and discuss your health history and questions with a licensed provider.

What happens if I stop the medication?

Experiences after stopping Semaglutide vary. Some people may notice changes in appetite, food cravings, blood-sugar patterns, or weight, while others may have a different experience.

Do not stop abruptly, taper, or change your dose on your own. Work with your licensed provider to discuss why you are considering stopping, whether a change is appropriate, and how your health and wellness plan should be monitored.

How do I take Semaglutide?

Semaglutide injection is generally administered subcutaneously once a week when prescribed. Your clinician and dispensing pharmacy should provide the exact dose, schedule, storage guidance, injection-site instructions, and administration training for your prescription.

Follow those instructions carefully. Do not share medication or supplies, and do not change the dose or frequency unless your licensed provider directs you to do so.
