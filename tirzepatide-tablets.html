What are Compound Tirzepatide Tablets?

Compounding involves creating a medication specifically tailored to an individual's needs. For instance, a patient might require an oral version of a medication that is only available as a tablet. Additionally, pharmacies can compound medications using FDA-approved drugs that are in short supply.

These compounded drugs are prepared by state-licensed pharmacies that adhere to both FDA and state regulations, ensuring they meet quality standards. When made according to federal laws, compounded drugs do not require FDA approval and are exempt from undergoing safety, effectiveness, or manufacturing reviews.

How do I take Tirzepatide tablets?

Compound Tirzepatide tablets are administered via daily doses under the tongue. They are a popular alternative for patients that are uncomfortable with self-injections. Sublingual medication works by placing the drug under the tongue, where it dissolves and is absorbed directly into the bloodstream through the tissues. The area under the tongue has a rich supply of blood vessels, which allows the medication to bypass the digestive system and liver.

The science behind Tirzepatide Tablets

When you eat, the food stimulates your gut to release the GLP-1 hormone. GLP-1 helps lower blood glucose (blood sugar) by triggering insulin release. Insulin helps blood glucose enter your body's cells for later energy use.

In some people, the gut doesn't make enough natural GLP-1, or the brain isn't sensitive to it.

Tirzepatide is a synthetic GLP-1 RA (receptor agonist). A drug that works as an agonist activates the same cell receptors as the body's natural hormones. Tirzepatide works in the gut like the body's natural GLP-1, slowing digestion, curbing hunger, and signaling fullness to the brain.

Prime Point supports a sustainable, flexible approach to nutrition rather than an unnecessarily restrictive diet. Your provider may discuss nourishing food choices, adequate protein, hydration, meal size, and eating patterns based on your goals, health history, and how you tolerate treatment.

Individual needs differ. Avoid making major dietary changes or beginning a highly restrictive eating plan solely because of Tirzepatide Tablets without first discussing it with your care team.

What side effects can occur with Tirzepatide Tablets?

Possible side effects may include:

  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Diarrhea
  • Constipation
  • Abdominal pain or discomfort
  • Decreased appetite
  • Indigestion

Side effects and their severity vary, and other risks may be serious. Contact your healthcare professional if symptoms persist, worsen, or concern you, and seek urgent care for severe symptoms. Review the prescribing information and discuss your health history and questions with a licensed provider.

What happens if I stop the medication?

Experiences after stopping Tirzepatide Tablets vary. Some people may notice changes in appetite, food cravings, blood-sugar patterns, or weight, while others may have a different experience.

Do not stop abruptly, taper, or change your dose on your own. Work with your licensed provider to discuss why you are considering stopping, whether a change is appropriate, and how your health and wellness plan should be monitored.

How do I take Tirzepatide Tablets?

Tirzepatide tablets are taken by mouth according to the exact dose, schedule, and administration directions supplied by your clinician and dispensing pharmacy. Instructions can be specific, including when to take the tablet in relation to food, beverages, and other medications.

Follow your prescription instructions carefully. Do not split, crush, share, or change the dose or frequency unless your licensed provider directs you to do so.
